# Legal briefing pack

For external counsel (EXT-02, with EXT-03 telecom and EXT-04 AI Act folded in).

**Nothing in this directory is a legal conclusion.** It describes what the product does and what we
believe about it, so counsel can tell us where we are wrong. Where we have taken a position, it is
labelled as ours and the conservative alternative is stated beside it.

| Document                                                       | For                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| [`01-product-and-data-flows.md`](01-product-and-data-flows.md) | What the product does, and where personal data goes |
| [`02-questions.md`](02-questions.md)                           | The decisions we need, with deadlines               |
| [`03-disclosure-and-scripts.md`](03-disclosure-and-scripts.md) | Draft caller-facing wording                         |

The inventory ([`../privacy/data-inventory.md`](../privacy/data-inventory.md)) and the data-flow
diagram ([`../architecture/data-flow.md`](../architecture/data-flow.md)) are part of the pack.

## Two stages

**Stage A — before a single real caller (PG-3).** The questions that decide whether the pilot may
run at all: the disclosure wording, the audio and turn-log position, the controller/processor
split, and the AVV.

**Stage B — before charging (P26).** Retention periods, the subprocessor register in final form,
the AI Act role, and the terms.

A stage A answer that is late stops the pilot. That is the correct behaviour, not a reason to
proceed without it.
